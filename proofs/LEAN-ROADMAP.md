# Lean Roadmap — what to kernel-check, in what order

Companion to [`../PROOF-ROADMAP.md`](../PROOF-ROADMAP.md). That file tracks
the whole verification stack; this one is only the Lean half: which claims
from the book and blog should become theorems, the statement each one
needs, and what it depends on. Everything here targets **core Lean 4, no
mathlib** (the `PflowProofs` lake project, toolchain `v4.33.0`), because
the point is that a reader can run `lake build` in under a minute and the
claims check. Items that genuinely need mathlib are marked and parked.

Two kinds of theorem appear, and they are not interchangeable:

- **G (general)** — quantifies over all nets. `invariant_lift` is one.
  These are the ones the book's sentences actually claim.
- **D (decide, per model)** — a concrete net, checked by `decide`/`rfl`.
  `pflow-polyglot` generates these from `model.json`. They are real
  theorems about *one* net and prove nothing about the construction.

A claim is only "verified" when its G-statement is proved, or when it is a
claim about one model and its D-statement is generated. Several items below
are "D now, G later" on purpose.

## Tier 1 — the sentences the book already says

These are claims in Appendix E or the Settle post that are stated as facts
and currently rest on nothing. Each is a small theorem on top of L1's
encoding (`Trans P`, `Net P`, `glue`).

| # | Claim (where) | Statement to prove | Kind | Depends on |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| L2 | **Firing is the incidence column.** `m' = m + C·e_t` (ch02, Appendix E, everywhere) | Define `fire t m := fun p => m p - pre t p + post t p` and `enabled t m := ∀ p, pre t p ≤ m p`; prove `fire t m = m + col t` pointwise and `enabled → ∀ p, 0 ≤ fire t m p` | G | — |
| L3 | **Invariants are conserved along runs.** `yᵀm` is constant on every reachable marking (this is what L0 checks per model by decide; L1 proves it lifts; nobody has proved it *follows from* `yᵀC = 0`) | `IsInvariant L N y → Reachable N m₀ m → weighted L y m = weighted L y m₀` with `Reachable` as the reflexive–transitive closure of firing | G | L2 |
| L4 | **Safety survives gluing** — Appendix E: "a property of the form 'no reachable marking looks like this' survives composition" | Restriction: every run of the glued net projects (along `f`) to a run of each part, hence a marking unreachable in a part cannot be reached in the composite with that projection | G | L2 |
| L5 | **Liveness does not survive gluing** — Appendix E; P2 found it empirically | A concrete counterexample: `N₁` with a transition live from `m₀`, the glued net with the same transition dead — as a D-theorem on two tiny nets (`decide` over a bounded reachable set) plus a G-statement that the implication *fails* (`¬ ∀ …`, witnessed by the example) | D + G | L2 |
| L6 | **Restriction, not extension** — P2's finding: a composite invariant restricts to each part; a part invariant does **not** extend by zero | Restriction as G (`IsInvariant M (glue …) y → IsInvariant L₁ N₁ (y ∘ f ∘ inl)`); non-extension as the P2 netting net, D | G + D | L1 |

L3–L4 are what turn L1 from "an algebraic identity lifts" into "the
*behavioural* consequence lifts", which is what the book means when it says
conservation composes.

## Tier 2 — the two boundaries

The August revision's central structural claim. Most of it is finite
combinatorics and is cheap to state.

| # | Claim | Statement | Kind | Depends on |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| L7 | **ρ > 1 breaks the event-graph property** (Appendix E, "Where the Free Structure Stops") | Define `rho t := Σ pre / Σ post`, `IsEventGraph N := ∀ p, producers p = 1 ∧ consumers p = 1`; prove a net containing a transition with ≥ 2 distinct input places of multiplicity 1 each and one output is not an event graph *unless* those places have no other consumer — i.e. state exactly the condition P2 measured ("breaks at the pending places") | G | — |
| L8 | **Gluing preserves the event-graph property iff no boundary place gains a second producer or consumer** — the honest form of "composition keeps the core clean" | `IsEventGraph N₁ → IsEventGraph N₂ → (∀ q, producers_glued q ≤ 1 ∧ consumers_glued q ≤ 1) → IsEventGraph (glue f N₁ N₂)`, and the Settle cycle as the D instance | G + D | L7 |
| L9 | **Read arc ≃ self-loop under interleaving semantics** (the VSY point as the Settle post now states it) | Extend `Trans` with `read : P → Nat`; define enablement with read; define the self-loop encoding `sl t` (add `read` to both pre and post); prove `Reachable N m₀ m ↔ Reachable (N.map sl) m₀ m` — the reachability sets coincide | G | L2, L3 |
| L10 | **…and not under partial-order semantics** | Out of scope for core Lean: needs unfoldings. Record as a cited result (VSY 1998), not a theorem. | — | — |
| L11 | **Inhibitor arcs have no self-loop encoding** | Small: exhibit an inhibitor net whose reachable set is not the reachable set of any ordinary net on the same places with the same initial marking — D on a 2-place net, by `decide` over all ordinary nets of that size (finite!) | D | L9 |

L9 is the one that matters: it is what licenses pulling the overdraft guard
into the ZK circuit. It is also the first theorem here that is about
*semantics* rather than linear algebra.

## Generation, not hand-writing

L0 set the rule and it should hold for every D-item: **a per-model theorem
is emitted by `tools/codegen`, never typed by hand.** Concretely, the
generator should grow these emitters as the tiers land:

- L5/L6/L11 counterexample nets: a `-lang lean` run on a model.json whose
  expected verdicts are declared in the model (a `claims:` block —
  `live: send_ab`, `unreachable: {b: 2}`) emits the matching D-theorems, so
  the same file that documents a claim is the one that checks it.
- L14: `λ` for event-graph models, emitted as a `decide` over enumerated
  cycles once the model is known to be an event graph (L8's D-check gates it).

The G-theorems live in `PflowProofs/` and are hand-written once; the
D-theorems are regenerated on every model change. `make check-generated` in
pflow-polyglot and `lake build` here are the two gates, and both must be in
CI before any Tier 2 item is called done.

## Order of work

1. **L2 → L3 → L4** (one file, `Semantics.lean`; a day). This is the
   conversion from algebra to behaviour and unblocks everything below.
2. **L6 + L5** (restriction and the two counterexamples; half a day, mostly
   reusing P2's nets).
3. **L7 → L8** (event graphs; half a day).
4. **L9** (read arcs; the first genuinely semantic theorem; a day or two).
5. **L12/L13** (Settle characterisation; the completeness direction is the
   hard part; two days).
6. **L14/L15** (cycle means; a day).
7. CI for both gates, then revisit L16 only if the book text demands it.
